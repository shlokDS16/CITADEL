# Traffic Rules, Violations & Challans (Citizen View)

Public, citizen-facing guidance about traffic rules, e-challans and how
to respond to a violation notice. Citizens may see fine amounts, how to
pay or dispute, and what documents to carry. Internal enforcement
operations, camera/officer assignments, raw evidence files and other
people's violation records are NOT citizen-accessible.

## Documents you must carry / keep valid

- Valid Driving Licence for the vehicle class.
- Vehicle Registration Certificate (RC).
- Valid third-party (or comprehensive) insurance.
- Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate.
Digital copies in DigiLocker or the mParivahan app are legally accepted.

## Common traffic violations

The CITADEL traffic system detects, among others:
- No helmet (two-wheeler rider/pillion without helmet)
- No seatbelt
- Over-speeding
- Red-light jumping (signal violation)
- Wrong-lane / wrong-side driving
- Triple riding
- Using mobile phone while driving
- Illegal / no-parking
- Driving without valid documents

### Fine amounts
Fine amounts are set by the competent authority under the Motor Vehicles
Act and state rules, and can change. This assistant always quotes the
**current fine configured in the CITADEL system** (the system reads the
live fines table), so the number you are told is always up to date. Ask
"what is the fine for <violation>" to get the current amount.

## How an e-challan works

1. A roadside / signal camera or an uploaded clip is analysed by the
   CITADEL traffic module.
2. A violation is detected, the vehicle number is read, and an incident
   is created for officer review.
3. Once an officer approves it, an e-challan is generated and a
   notification (including the evidence frame) is sent to the registered
   contact.
4. The challan shows the violation type, location/camera, date-time,
   the fine amount, and the due date.

## Paying a challan

- Pay online through the official state traffic-police / Parivahan
  e-challan portal using the challan number or vehicle number.
- In the CITADEL notification you may get a direct "Pay now" option;
  confirming it records your payment and marks the challan PAID.
- Always keep the payment receipt. Paying before the due date avoids
  additional late penalty or court referral.

## Disputing a challan

If you believe a challan is wrong (wrong vehicle, already paid, unclear
evidence, emergency, etc.):
- Choose the "Dispute / Contest" option in the notification, or contest
  it through the official traffic-police grievance / Lok Adalat process.
- The case is flagged for officer re-review; you may be asked for
  documents or to appear before a traffic magistrate / Lok Adalat.
- Do not ignore a challan — unpaid challans can lead to higher penalty,
  licence action, or a court summons.

## Repeat offences

Repeated violations on the same vehicle can mean escalated fines,
licence suspension, or mandatory counselling/retest as per law. A
citizen can see their own pending challans and history; they cannot see
other citizens' records.

## Your rights & safe practices

- You can ask for the evidence basis of your own challan.
- Wear helmets/seatbelts, follow signals and lane discipline, never
  drink and drive, and keep documents valid — most fines are fully
  avoidable.
- Keep your mobile number updated with the RTO so you receive challan
  notifications.

## What is NOT available to citizens here

For privacy and security, this assistant will politely decline requests
for: another person's challans or vehicle/owner details, internal camera
locations and officer assignments, raw enforcement evidence archives,
and internal audit logs. Citizens can only access their own information
through the official authenticated channels.
